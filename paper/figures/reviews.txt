KG Voyager: Knowledge Graph Agnostic Question Answering via Agentic Navigation
Download PDF
Essam Wisam, Chengkai Li 
26 May 2026 (modified: 01 Jul 2026)
ACL ARR 2026 May Submission
Everyone
Revisions

Keywords: knowledge graph question answering, KGQA, SPARQL, RDF, text-to-SPARQL, LLM agents, agentic navigation, semantic parsing, domain-specific knowledge graphs
Abstract:
Knowledge Graph Question Answering (KGQA) over RDF graphs remains challenging in domain-specific settings, where formal ontologies and curated text–SPARQL pairs are often unavailable. We present KGVoyager, a KG-agnostic agentic architecture that generates SPARQL queries from natural language questions by dynamically discovering graph structure and semantics, requiring only a query endpoint of the underlying graph. Using a think–act–observe loop with search, exploration, and execution tools, KGVoyager maps terms to graph IRIs, uncovers structure, and refines queries through execution feedback—all without pre-existing ontologies or examples. Unlike the prior state of the art, KGVoyager requires only a lightweight class index which renders it applicable for far more real-world endpoints. Across four benchmarks, KGVoyager improves F1 by 
8 points while cutting cost and runtime by 
22% each.

Official Review of Submission16054 by Reviewer fPxe
Official Reviewby Reviewer fPxe06 Jul 2026, 15:28 (modified: 08 Jul 2026, 07:59)Program Chairs, Senior Area Chairs, Area Chairs, Reviewers Submitted, Authors, Reviewer fPxeRevisions
Paper Summary:
The paper addresses question answering over arbitrary RDF knowledge graphs where only a SPARQL endpoint is available and no ontology documentation, text–SPARQL demonstrations, or pre-built entity index can be assumed. The proposed system, KGVoyager, is a ReAct-style agent that dynamically discovers the structure of an unknown knowledge graph using a suite of search, exploration, and execution tools before constructing a SPARQL query. The key design decision is replacing the large entity index required by GRASP with a lightweight class index combined with on-demand instance lookup and empirical property exploration. The system is evaluated on four domain-specific benchmarks using four backbone LLMs and is compared primarily against GRASP. The authors further present an endpoint feasibility study over 29 real-world Open Knowledge Network endpoints demonstrating that class indexing is substantially more deployable than entity indexing. The paper positions its contribution primarily as a practical systems improvement for enterprise KGQA rather than a new learning algorithm. Overall, I believe I understood the intended contribution correctly: the novelty lies in making agentic KGQA applicable to previously unseen SPARQL endpoints with minimal assumptions through careful system design rather than through a fundamentally new retrieval or reasoning algorithm.

Summary Of Strengths:
S1. The paper addresses a genuinely important deployment scenario that has received relatively little attention. Much previous work assumes complete ontologies, manually curated demonstrations, or public KGs such as Wikidata or DBpedia. Supporting arbitrary enterprise RDF endpoints using only endpoint access is a realistic and practically valuable goal.

S2. The endpoint feasibility study across 29 Open Knowledge Network endpoints is one of the strongest aspects of the paper. Rather than merely claiming broader applicability, the authors empirically demonstrate that entity-index construction frequently fails while lightweight class indexing succeeds on the majority of tested endpoints. This directly supports the central motivation behind the proposed architecture and provides evidence beyond standard benchmark comparisons.

S3. Class-level retrieval, class-scoped instance lookup, empirical property usage exploration, iterative query execution, and execution feedback naturally complement each other. The design choices are individually motivated and the running DBLP example clearly illustrates how the agent incrementally discovers an unknown schema before constructing the final SPARQL query.

Summary Of Weaknesses:
W1. The evaluation consists of approximately 220 questions in total, with some datasets being extremely small (e.g., DREAM-KG contains only 14 questions). Although results are averaged across three runs, the paper reports neither standard deviations nor statistical significance tests. Consequently, it is difficult to determine whether several reported improvements—particularly the smaller gains in DBLP or the relatively small ablation differences—are statistically meaningful. Reporting variance would substantially strengthen the empirical evidence.

W2. The empirical comparison focuses almost exclusively on GRASP because it shares the endpoint-only setting. While this is understandable, the paper does not sufficiently position itself relative to broader agentic KGQA systems such as Think-on-Graph (ToG) or Plan-onGraph(PoG). Although ToG assumes local graph deployment, it remains one of the closest reasoning-based approaches in the literature. Either an empirical comparison under adapted settings or a deeper discussion of expected differences in assumptions, efficiency, and reasoning capabilities would better contextualize the contribution.

W3. No analysis of reasoning complexity or multi-hop performance. One of the main motivations of KGVoyager is iterative exploration of previously unseen knowledge graphs. Surprisingly, the evaluation never analyzes question complexity. Results are not broken down by reasoning depth, hop count, number of joins, aggregation operations, or compositional complexity. Consequently, it remains unclear whether the proposed exploration strategy provides its greatest benefit on genuinely difficult multi-hop reasoning problems or primarily on relatively simple schema discovery tasks.

W4. The running example requires approximately 10 tool calls to answer a relatively simple query. Although KGVoyager assumes only endpoint access whereas systems such as Think-on-Graph (ToG) operate under different assumptions, a comparison of reasoning efficiency (e.g., number of reasoning/tool steps or endpoint queries) would better position the proposed approach. Such an analysis would help readers understand the trade-off between the broader applicability of KGVoyager and the additional interaction cost introduced by dynamic schema exploration.

W5. Missing error analysis. The paper reports aggregate F1 scores but does not investigate failure modes. It would be informative to categorize errors into incorrect entity resolution, incorrect property selection, failed schema exploration, SPARQL construction errors, endpoint failures, or reasoning mistakes. Such an analysis would help explain where the proposed agent succeeds and where future improvements should focus.

W6. Several implementation details affecting reproducibility are omitted. Several important implementation choices are absent from the paper itself. In particular, the adaptive filtering threshold (δ), dense embedding model, retrieval parameters, Reciprocal Rank Fusion configuration, caching strategy, and vector indexing details are not explicitly reported. These design choices directly affect retrieval quality and runtime and should be documented within the paper.

Comments Suggestions And Typos:
Report per-dataset standard deviations and statistical significance tests alongside the average F1 scores.

Include a discussion or comparison with Think-on-Graph (ToG), even if only qualitatively, to better position the work relative to recent agentic KGQA literature.

Analyze performance as a function of reasoning complexity (e.g., hop count, joins, aggregation, compositional questions).

Report the distribution of tool invocations per question together with endpoint query counts and latency.

Add an error analysis identifying the dominant failure modes of the agent.

Explicitly report implementation parameters including δ, embedding model, retrieval configuration, vector index, and caching strategy.

Clarify how runtime scales with endpoint latency and graph size.

Official Reviewby Reviewer ZvBS01 Jul 2026, 23:07 (modified: 08 Jul 2026, 07:59)Program Chairs, Senior Area Chairs, Area Chairs, Reviewers Submitted, Authors, Reviewer ZvBSRevisions
Paper Summary:
This paper presents KGVoyager, an agent-based KGQA system for generating SPARQL queries over RDF knowledge graphs when the user only has access to a SPARQL endpoint.

Summary Of Strengths:
The paper targets a limitation of existing KGQA systems: many domain-specific RDF graphs do not come with well-documented ontologies or enough text-SPARQL training examples

The tool design is technically sensible.

Summary Of Weaknesses:
The claim of being “KG-agnostic” is somewhat overstated. The method still assumes that the endpoint exposes classes and instance membership through standard RDFS/RDF typing patterns. This means the method is better described as RDFS-compatible endpoint-only KGQA, rather than fully KG-agnostic KGQA.

The benchmark scale is too small. DREAM-KG has only 14 questions, Climate Models KG has 52, and SOCKG has 54. Even DBLP-QuAD is evaluated on only a random sample of 100 test questions.

The baseline comparison is too narrow and the ablations are limited. GRASP is the most relevant baseline, but the paper would be stronger with additional comparisons, such as a direct LLM text-to-SPARQL baseline using only endpoint-provided schema snippets, a ReAct agent without the proposed exploration tools, and possibly a schema-aware baseline where partial ontology information is available. Without these, it is hard to isolate whether the gain comes from the proposed tool suite, from prompting, from retrieval, or from execution-based self-correction. They are run only with Gemini 3 Flash Preview and mainly on SOCKG/DBLP. The paper should test the ablations across more models and datasets.

Please see the weakness part.

Paper Summary:
The paper proposes KGVoyager, an endpoint-only Knowledge Graph Question Answering (KGQA) agent that generates SPARQL queries over arbitrary RDF knowledge graphs without curated ontologies or text-SPARQL examples. Its key idea is to replace GRASP’s complete entity index with a lightweight class-level index, and to use search, exploration, and execution tools in a think-act-observe loop. Experiments show around 8-point F1 improvement over GRASP, with lower cost and runtime.

Summary Of Strengths:
The problem is well motivated. Endpoint-only KGQA is practical and important for domain-specific knowledge graphs.
The feasibility study is strong. The class-index vs. entity-index comparison gives concrete evidence for the main design choice.
The tool design is clear and sensible. The running example helps explain the workflow.
The evaluation covers multiple benchmarks and backbone models, and the comparison against GRASP under its best-case setting is relatively fair.
Summary Of Weaknesses:
The empirical evidence is still limited. Some benchmarks are very small, but no variance, confidence interval, or significance test is reported.
The main F1 comparison is conditional on GRASP’s entity index being available, while the paper’s motivation is that this index is often unavailable.
Some analysis is over-scoped. Tool-use dynamics are shown for only two models, and all ablations are conducted with one model.
The robustness of key hyperparameters, such as the adaptive filtering threshold and sampling size, is not studied.
The claimed KG-agnostic applicability still depends on RDFS-style class/type conventions.
Comments Suggestions And Typos:
Please report standard deviations or confidence intervals for the three-run averages, especially because DREAM-KG has only 14 questions.
Please clarify that the F1 comparison with GRASP is under GRASP’s best-case setting with an available entity index, not a pure endpoint-only comparison.
Please test whether the tool-use dynamics and ablation conclusions hold across more backbone models, or scope the claims accordingly.
Please add sensitivity analysis for the adaptive filtering threshold and the property-usage sampling size.
Please clarify the RDFS assumption more carefully. The system is KG-agnostic only for graphs that expose compatible class/type structures.
